**What it shows:** layout shift isn't the hard part, and these sites miss LCP on media and TBT on script. Only the plainest page clears all three lines. A signature moment needn't block the page if it loads late: the Pudding piece has no blocking time, though it misses the LCP line, and nothing after load was measured.

**Still not measured:** INP and any field data; anything on a real phone; anything after load, such as scrolling, interaction or frame rate during motion; and the other showcase sites. A lab run can't tell us how these sites feel. The jury scores in section 1 are opinions, not measurements (WPO is web performance optimization, Resp. is responsive design, A11y is accessibility).

## What piece 1 and the phone tests taught us

Sources: the plan's change log and G34, [`route-transitions.md`](route-transitions.md), [`ios-chrome-view-transitions.md`](ios-chrome-view-transitions.md), [ADR 0001](../adr/0001-astro-over-next.md), and the essay template.

- **`<ClientRouter />` runs the transition, so pages swap without a reload.** Scripts keep running through swaps, while a React island is unmounted unless it's persisted. Every effect must clean up on unmount and have a test that reaches it from another page. In Astro 7.4.4 that happens through `astro:unmount`, so `useEffect` cleanup is the place for it. Effects with no listeners at all, like CSS animations, have nothing to clean up, but still need the test.
- **Essay pages are the card's color,** because the card grows into the page. A treatment works on `--paper-2`, in light and dark. It can't bring its own page background without a color jump when the card lands.
- **The card's text only moves.** The kicker, title and dek keep their width and line breaks from card to page. Different metrics on the two sides would break that (inferred; a font change hasn't been tried).
- **The parade stays out of the transition** until the card lands. WebKit also drops `backdrop-filter` during a transition, which is why its bar pops in Safari.
- **The phone decides feel.** The throttled checks reported no layout shift for a jump Josh could see, and the Simulator can't run Chrome on iOS. Checks catch regressions. Contact sheets catch obvious faults. Josh signs off motion on his phone (`docs/agents/local-dev.md`).
- **Chrome on iOS is WebKit underneath,** so iOS support below is the same for both browsers. Its own glitches are separate, and can't be seen without a phone.
- **Lab numbers are noisy.** Lighthouse LCP swung by up to about 1,200 ms between runs of one page in the bake-off. Headless Chromium draws WebGL in software, so its frame rates say nothing about a phone.
- **Our own baseline, on deployed previews:** essay LCP 1,402 ms and TBT 2 ms, heavy page LCP 1,334 ms and TBT 0 ms, INP 40 ms and 72 ms (ADR 0001, from the bake-off).

## 2. Treatment catalog

Support was checked on 2026-11-01. Percentages are from [caniuse's data](https://github.com/Fyrd/caniuse) (last commit 2026-10-29), and browser versions from [MDN's compat data](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data). Baseline labels are from each MDN page. Current stable: Chrome 156, Firefox 159, Safari 27.2.

Universal a11y rule: gate motion with `prefers-reduced-motion` ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion); [caniuse 97.2%](https://caniuse.com/prefers-reduced-motion)) and replace rather than remove ([Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/react/prefers-reduced-motion/)).

1. **Scroll-driven animations** (reveals, progress bars, pinned sequences via `animation-timeline: scroll()/view()`). Seen: Comeau writes on it ([site](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/)). **Support: now in all three engines:** Chrome and Edge 115, Safari and iOS 26, and [Firefox 159](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Mozilla/Firefox/Releases/159), released 2026-10-27 ([release notes](https://www.firefox.com/en-US/firefox/159.0/releasenotes/)). The labels haven't caught up: [MDN's page](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/animation-timeline) (modified Sep 16, 2026) still says it isn't Baseline, and so does the [web-features data](https://github.com/web-platform-dx/web-features/blob/main/features/scroll-driven-animations.yml.dist), which lists no Firefox version. MDN's compat data does list Firefox 159. Treat it as progressive enhancement behind `@supports`, since Firefox's support is brand new and iOS before 26 has none. **Threading: confirmed.** WebKit's own [Safari 26.4 post](https://webkit.org/blog/17862/webkit-features-for-safari-26-4/) (Mar 24, 2026) says these animations "run on the compositor thread, separate from the main thread". [Technology Preview 234's notes](https://webkit.org/blog/17674/release-notes-for-safari-technology-preview-234/) limit that to `opacity`, `transform`, `translate`, `scale`, `rotate`, `filter`, `backdrop-filter` and Motion Path. That rests on WebKit's word: I found no independent measurement and ran nothing on a phone. Apple's 26.4 release notes page wouldn't load for my tools. Cost: animate those properties only. A11y: wrap in `no-preference`; content must read fine without it.
2. **View Transitions** (page-to-page morphs, shared hero image). **Our card transition is this one,** same-document through `<ClientRouter />` (G34). Support: same-document is [Baseline 2025](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Document/startViewTransition) ([caniuse 92.9%](https://caniuse.com/view-transitions)). Cross-document, which we no longer use: Chrome 126, Safari 18.2, and no Firefox in [MDN's data](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@view-transition) ([caniuse 86.8%](https://caniuse.com/cross-document-view-transitions)). Cost: one snapshot per named element, so name few ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/View_Transition_API)). Snapshots are flattened in WebKit, and it clips above the toolbar ([details](ios-chrome-view-transitions.md)). A11y: the layout skips the transition under `reduce`; the API avoids duplicate-DOM focus and live-region problems that hand-built transitions cause.
3. **Layered, color-matched shadows for depth.** Seen: Comeau's [shadow guide](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/css/designing-shadows/). Support: universal. Cost: each layer adds paint work, "5x more work"; don't animate shadows, fade a pseudo-element's opacity instead ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide)). A11y: shadows can't carry meaning; keep text contrast.
4. **Layered parallax with CSS 3D transforms** (`perspective` + `translateZ`, no scroll handlers). Seen: GSAP-driven sites such as [Jesper Landberg](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/jesper-landberg-4); the pure-CSS route is from [Chrome's guide](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/performant-parallaxing), and item 1 now does this job more simply. Support: 3D transforms are Baseline widely available ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/transform)). Cost: avoid scroll listeners and `background-position`; known Safari and Firefox sticky quirks. A11y: parallax is a classic vestibular trigger, so flatten fully under `reduce`.
5. **SVG line drawing and morphing** (`stroke-dashoffset`). Seen: Comeau's SVG micro-interactions ([site](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/)). How: [CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/svg-line-animation-works/); use `pathLength` to normalize. Support: universal. Cost: tiny for a few paths, expensive for hundreds of nodes. Not on WebKit's threaded list above. A11y: give the SVG `role="img"` with a title, or `aria-hidden` if decorative; show the finished drawing under `reduce`.
6. **Canvas/WebGL accents** (one hero scene, not a whole site). Seen: [Bruno Simon](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/brunos-portfolio), [Lando Norris](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/lando-norris), [Heckel](https://blog.maximeheckel.com/), and our heavy page. Support: [WebGL2 97.1%](https://caniuse.com/webgl2), [OffscreenCanvas 96.3%](https://caniuse.com/offscreencanvas), Baseline since March 2023 with [some parts varying](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/OffscreenCanvas). Cost: highest of all; dynamic-`import()` the code after first paint, render in a worker via [OffscreenCanvas](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/OffscreenCanvas) where you can, pause when off-screen, cap pixel ratio. A11y: Bruno's A11y jury scores are 6.6–7.0; keep an HTML text equivalent, a pause control, a static poster under `reduce`.
7. **Container queries** (art-direct a figure or card by its own width). Seen: Fisher's responsive experiments, where she says she wants container queries ([web.dev](https://web.dev/community-highlight-lynn-fisher/)). Support: [95.8%](https://caniuse.com/css-container-queries), Chrome 105, Firefox 110, Safari 16 ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/CSS_containment/Container_queries)). Cost: small; `container-type: inline-size` applies containment. A11y: don't shrink text below readable; cqi units follow the container, not user zoom, so keep a `rem` floor.
8. **Variable fonts with fallback matching** (expressive weight/width, one file). No showcase site verified. Support: [97.2%](https://caniuse.com/variable-fonts); [`font-display`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@font-face/font-display) is widely available ([97.2%](https://caniuse.com/css-font-rendering-controls)), and [`size-adjust`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@font-face/size-adjust) since September 2023. Cost: one file beats many weights only if you use many styles; use WOFF2, subset with `unicode-range`, preload only the key face, tune the fallback with `size-adjust` to protect CLS ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/font-best-practices)). A11y: honor user zoom and `rem` units; avoid ultra-thin weights for body copy.
9. **Spring and bounce easing with `linear()`** (playful hover and entry motion, no JS). Seen: Comeau ([site](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/)). Support: [widely available](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/easing-function/linear) since December 2023. Cost: compositor-friendly when applied to `transform`. A11y: apply only inside `no-preference`.
10. **`@starting-style` entry animations** (fade/slide in popovers, dialogs, inserted nodes). Seen: Comeau covers it ([site](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/)). Support: [Baseline 2024](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@starting-style), newly available since August 2024. Cost: negligible. A11y: native `<dialog>`/popover keep focus handling; add the reduced-motion override.
11. **Scrollytelling with IntersectionObserver** (sticky chart that changes per paragraph). Seen: [The Pudding](https://pudding.cool/) via [Scrollama](https://github.com/russellgoldenberg/scrollama). Support: universal. Cost: observers avoid scroll-event jank; keep the sticky layer a single compositor layer. A11y: write the prose as real headings and paragraphs so the story is complete without the graphic; provide text alternatives for charts; use `position: sticky`, not hijacked scroll.
12. **Image and loading strategy** (the LCP guard for every treatment above). Preload or inline the hero in HTML, use `fetchpriority="high"` on it, never lazy-load it, and lazy-load everything else ([web.dev LCP](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-lcp); [lazy loading 96.4%](https://caniuse.com/loading-lazy-attr)). Add `width`/`height` to prevent CLS. Use [`content-visibility: auto`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/content-visibility) (Baseline 2024, [94.9%](https://caniuse.com/css-content-visibility)) for long essays, but not around a sticky section or anything the parade measures (inferred; untested). A11y: meaningful `alt`; empty `alt` for decoration.
13. **Per-essay interactive widgets, code-split.** Seen: Comeau builds a custom widget for nearly every post ([summary](https://yespress.io/josh-w-comeau)). Cost: load each widget's JS only on its page, on visibility, with Astro's [`client:visible`](https://docs.astro.build/en/reference/directives-reference/), which takes a `rootMargin` so hydration starts early. Support: dynamic `import()` is Baseline widely available ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/JavaScript/Reference/Operators/import)). Keep handlers short for INP ≤ 200 ms ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/inp)). A11y: real buttons and inputs, visible focus, keyboard operable.

## 3. What this means for phase 3

**The stance holds:** plain semantic HTML as the base, one signature moment per piece, CSS-only polish elsewhere, and nothing that has to be listened to if CSS can do it.

### Piece 2: the chart playground

Everything here is a cheap prototype on the card's color, with the S16 accessibility pattern, and anything that needs JavaScript loads with `client:visible`. A write-up has no parade, so there's no bar to fit around.

- **Scroll-driven robot: CSS only.** A `view()` timeline on `transform` and `opacity`, with stepped keyframes for the cranium snapping shut and the switch turning on. Scrolling stays native, it's threaded in Safari 26.4 and later, and with no listeners there's nothing to clean up across swaps, so it may need no client directive. It still needs #55's test from another page. Without `@supports` or under `reduce`, show the finished robot. **Check on the phone first:** nothing says how a `view()` range behaves as the iOS toolbar collapses.
- **Umbrella carousel: CSS 3D with real buttons.** The real buttons sit outside the `aria-hidden` visual, placed over its panels, because S16 never puts a focusable element inside `aria-hidden`. Clicking one runs a CSS transition on `transform`, snapping under `reduce`. Size it with container queries so it fits a phone, and don't rely on hover. Don't use a view transition for the twist: [starting one skips any in flight](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-view-transitions-1/), including the router's. It's first on the cut list, so build it last.
- **Pixel-art battle: SVG rects and `steps()`.** It runs on time, not scroll, so it starts when visible, stops off screen, clears its timers on unmount, and has a real pause or step button: WCAG asks for [a way to pause, stop or hide](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html) motion that starts on its own and lasts over five seconds. Its still state is the final menu.
- **Mermaid floor: commit the SVGs.** Generate the text from the run file. [rehype-mermaid](https://github.com/remcohaszing/rehype-mermaid) needs Playwright and a browser to render outside one, and its other strategy renders on the client, which S16 rules out. So by the spec's rule, the rendered SVGs are committed and a test checks they match the run file. A committed SVG needs to work in both themes, through CSS-variable colors or two renders, so #53 decides that and records the departure from S16's build-time rendering as a planning decision. Check that labels fit, since Mermaid sizes them with the fonts on the machine that rendered them (inferred).

### Piece 3: the recommendation

**One quiet page in one new font, with the Swirl as its only moment.** The Swirl is the treatment's reason to exist, and it's enough. It goes in unchanged on Canvas 2D (G35), and everything below is what shipping it needs.

**What this leaves untested, for Josh to decide:** the plan calls piece 3 a heavy treatment to stress-test the stack, and this is a quiet one. Keeping the card's text in the house font also means phase 3 never tries a treatment font on a title during the transition, so phase 5 would meet that question without evidence. The harm it avoids is inferred, not seen.

- **Color:** keep `--paper-2` and `--ink` as they are, at about 13.9:1 in both light and dark, plus the existing accent (4.6:1 in light, 7.4:1 in dark, by my calculation from `site.css`). A treatment's color can't change the page behind the card.
- **Font: Bricolage Grotesque, variable, weight axis only, subset to the characters the piece uses.** Stated reason (S5): the Swirl draws each letter rotated and shrinking, so the piece needs a sturdy, low-contrast face, where the placeholder serif's thin strokes would break up at small sizes (my judgment, not a test). The subset must include ň and ť for Haňťa, which Fontsource's latin file leaves out and its latin-ext range covers, so check the glyphs are there. Those two WOFF2 files from [Fontsource](https://www.jsdelivr.com/package/npm/@fontsource-variable/bricolage-grotesque) 5.3.0 are 41 KB and 19 KB, so one subset file should come in under 60 KB. It's under the [OFL](https://github.com/ateliertriay/bricolage), so it can be self-hosted in this public repo. **I haven't seen it in the Swirl.** Try it there first: if it fails on the phone, change the font, not the Swirl.
- **The card's text stays in the house font.** The kicker, title and dek keep their font on piece 3, and the new one starts below them. The morph then can't depend on a font file arriving, and the home page doesn't pay for a font it barely uses. The router preloads a new page's stylesheets before the swap, but not its font files, so a font on the title could swap as the card lands (from Astro 7.4.4's `router.js`; the last step is inferred).
- **Font loading:** `font-display: swap`, a fallback tuned with `size-adjust`, and a preload on piece 3 only. Canvas text doesn't load a font by itself, so wait on [`document.fonts.load()`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/CSS_Font_Loading_API) and redraw.
- **No layout shift from the upgrade:** the section's height and the sticky stage come from CSS, under `@media (scripting: enabled) and (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)` ([widely available](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/scripting)), so hydrating changes nothing. Everyone else gets plain paragraphs. Keep them real, in the MDX, passed to the island as children. Astro hands those over as finished HTML, so the Swirl reads its letters from the DOM. The paragraphs stay visible until the first draw, so a slow or failed bundle never leaves a blank tall section. #57 settles the MDX verdict (S15) first with a small one-off of its own, since the Swirl comes later (#62).
- **Canvas needs:** it doesn't inherit color, so read `--ink` and redraw when the color scheme changes. Cap the pixel ratio. Draw once on mount as well as on scroll, since Back restores a scrolled page. Letter positions are a pure function of scroll, so that frame is right. Clean up on unmount, with a test from another page.
- **With the parade:** the stage's height is the viewport minus `--bar-height`, so the bar never covers the spiral. The parade's `ResizeObserver` already handles the font swap and the tall section. Its fill counts the section's scrolling, so put the section between two first mentions, not at the end, and no first mention goes inside it. Sticky also needs no `overflow` clipping on its ancestors.
- **Threaded scroll animations don't help here.** Canvas drawing is on the main thread, so the throttled checks (INP, TBT) watch it for regressions and Josh's phone decides how it feels.

**Out of scope:** comparing spiral techniques, tuning the pacing, or making it reusable. All wait until after V1 (G35).
